MATCH MODE · COMPLETE RULEBOOK V9

A high difficulty 3v3 soccer strategy mode. Control Soccer, break defensive lines, decide when to attack, and manage every card during a 10-minute match.

1. Team Setup

Each team has three positions: Defender 1 (D1), Defender 2 (D2), and Goalkeeper (GK). Identities are public. Players receive the same number of cards. Before the match, teammates may exchange cards one-for-one. Extra cards are discarded so every player starts with equal cards.

2. Starting The Match

Rock-Paper-Scissors decides the first Soccer holder. The player holding Soccer controls possession and decides the attack.

3. Possession And Passing

The Soccer holder may PASS to any teammate without using a card. Passing does not skip the opponent's defensive ability.

PASS example:
Player A passes Soccer to teammate B. Opponent may use TACKLE during the pass window. If no successful TACKLE happens, Soccer reaches teammate B.

4. Successful TACKLE And Turnover

A successful TACKLE immediately steals Soccer.

Result:
The previous attack ends. All previous DRIBBLE progress, chase decisions, and defensive positions are cleared. The new attack always starts from Defender 1.

5. SHOOT Attack Flow

SHOOT → Defender 1 → Defender 2 → Goalkeeper

Every SHOOT must pass through the defensive lines in this order.

Defender 1

D1 may choose:

DEFENSE: Stops this SHOOT. Soccer remains with the attacking team. No turnover occurs.

TACKLE: Stops the SHOOT and steals Soccer. The defender becomes the new attacker.

No action: The attack continues to D2.

Defender 2

D2 follows the same choices as D1.

Important:
If D2 does not play DEFENSE or TACKLE, D2 does not defend. The attacker does not need DRIBBLE and immediately reaches the Goalkeeper.

6. DRIBBLE PAST

DRIBBLE is used to beat a defensive line that actually blocks the attack with DEFENSE or TACKLE.

Example:
D1 uses DEFENSE → attacker plays DRIBBLE → D1 is beaten.

After being beaten, that defender may choose:

1. Chase back by discarding one card and attempt another defense.

2. Allow the attack to continue to the next defensive line.

7. YELLOW CARD

YELLOW belongs to the current defensive line only.

D1 YELLOW can only be used while the attack is against D1. D2 YELLOW can only be used while the attack is against D2.

YELLOW can cancel DRIBBLE or an opponent YELLOW. It cannot cancel SHOOT or DEFENSE.

Example:
SHOOT → D1 DEFENSE → DRIBBLE → only D1 may respond with YELLOW.

After reaching D2, only D2 controls the YELLOW window.

8. Goalkeeper Defense

When SHOOT reaches GK:

If GK has no DEFENSE, the shot is a GOAL.

If GK plays DEFENSE, both sides play Rock-Paper-Scissors.

GK wins = SAVE. Shooter wins = GOAL.

9. Goalkeeper Attack

When GK controls Soccer, the goalkeeper may attack like any other player.

GK may PASS or SHOOT.

10. Goalkeeper Out Of Position

If GK attacks and loses Soccer because of TACKLE, the goalkeeper leaves the goal.

When the opponent attacks back and reaches GK:

GK may discard one card to RETURN TO GOAL.

This card only returns GK. It does not defend the shot.

After returning, GK still needs DEFENSE to attempt a save.

11. Complete Defense Examples

SHOOT → D1 DEFENSE → attack stops → Soccer stays with attacker.
SHOOT → D1 TACKLE → defender steals Soccer → new attack starts from D1.
SHOOT → D1 passed → D2 does nothing → no DRIBBLE required → GK challenge.
GK attacks → opponent TACKLE → GK leaves goal → must return before defending.

12. Match Ending

The match ends when the 10-minute timer expires, or when neither team can create a valid attack.

One team losing all SHOOT cards does not automatically end the match if the opponent can still attack.

13. Penalty Shootout

If the match is tied, use a real soccer style penalty shootout.

Five rounds are played. Each attempt:

Shooter draws one card. Opposing goalkeeper draws one new card.

SHOOT + no DEFENSE = GOAL.

SHOOT + DEFENSE = SAVE.

No SHOOT = MISS.

After five rounds, the higher score wins. If tied, continue with sudden death.

14. Final Reminder

Match Mode rewards decision making. Passing, defending, risking a goalkeeper attack, and saving cards for the right moment are all important.
